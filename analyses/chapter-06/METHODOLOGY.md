Working title: **"The Map of Crime Is Not the Map of Danger."** Every number
in the published chapter reproduces from `analyses/chapter-06/notebook.py`
against the pinned `MANIFEST.json` (`make chapter-6`). This file is the full

## The headline question

*Safety is broader than crime statistics.* New Yorkers face several
different kinds of physical harm, generated by different forces. Do they
concentrate in the same neighborhoods? Answer: **no.** Across the 59
residential community districts the three harms rank-correlate only weakly —
the crime map and the traffic map are different maps drawn on the same city.

## Spine test (the chapter's kill criterion)

Three harm axes, each per 1,000 residents, Spearman ρ over n = 59 CDs. The
series rule: if any pair co-ranks |ρ| ≥ 0.75 the spine collapses to fewer
axes. It doesn't:

| pair | Spearman ρ | |
|------|-----------:|--|
| violent ~ traffic | **+0.478** | different maps |
| violent ~ property | **+0.610** | the at-risk pair — shares the NYPD reporting pipeline — but stays distinct |
| traffic ~ property | **+0.447** | different maps |

All three axes ship. The property/violent pair is the one that could have
merged (both are NYPD complaints, same reporting-and-recording pipeline);
at +0.610 it stays two axes.

| Role | Source | Dataset / query | Window |
|------|--------|-----------------|--------|
| Violent crime | NYPD Complaint Data (Historic) | Socrata `qgea-i56i`, `law_cat_cd='FELONY'`, `ky_cd in (101,104,105,106)` = murder, rape, robbery, felony assault | 2022-01-01 .. 2024-12-31 |

| Population | ACS 2019–2023 5-year | `B01003` (tract) | — |
| Geography | NYC DCP Community Districts | `5crt-au7u` (59 residential CDs) | — |

Volumes: **373,531** major-felony complaints geocoded to a CD (2,292 lacked
coordinates and were dropped); **38,844** pedestrian/cyclist-harm crashes
(281 dropped), totaling **40,318 casualties and 379 deaths** over the window.
The seven categories above are NYPD's own "seven major felonies" index.

**Vintage exception.** The series is otherwise pinned to a 2026-06-01
snapshot, but crime and crashes are *event streams*: a single year is noisy
at CD resolution. We use a **fixed three-year window (2022–2024)** and
**annualize** (÷ 3) so per-CD rates are stable. No pre/post split; no trend
claim is made from a three-year window.

## Geography & population

- Spatial math in **EPSG:2263** (State Plane, feet); output in EPSG:4326.
- Complaints carry State-Plane `x_coord_cd`/`y_coord_cd`; crashes carry
  `latitude`/`longitude`. Each event is assigned to the CD containing its
  point (`geopandas.sjoin`, `within`). Null-island (0,0) crash coordinates
  are dropped.
- CD population = sum of tract `B01003_001E` over tracts whose
  representative point falls in the CD (22 of 2,325 tracts are water/edge).

## Metrics

- **Violent crime** = (murder + rape + robbery + felony assault) ÷ pop ÷ 3
  × 1,000. Range **0.99 – 16.83** per 1,000/yr; median **4.84**.
- **Traffic harm (KI)** = (pedestrian + cyclist casualties) ÷ pop ÷ 3 ×
  1,000. Range **0.58 – 6.24**; median **1.52**. Casualties = **killed OR
  injured** — the public collision data has no injury-severity grade, so a
  strict Vision-Zero **KSI** (killed or *severe* injury) cut is not
  possible; fatalities are carried separately (`traffic_killed_per100k`).
- **Property crime** = (burglary + grand larceny + GLA-motor-vehicle) ÷ pop
  ÷ 3 × 1,000. Range **3.65 – 64.95**; median **8.34**. Carried as a
  descriptive third axis; the *bodily-harm* story is violent + traffic.

## Traffic exposure cross-check

Per-resident overstates dense cores, where the crash toll is driven by
pedestrian *exposure*, not residents (Midtown has few residents and enormous
daytime foot traffic). We cross-check against the Vision-Zero exposure
denominator, **centerline street-miles** (CSCL, ~5,878 mi citywide),
distributed to CDs via the area-weighted `geographies/zip_cd_crosswalk.csv`.
Traffic KI **per-1,000-residents** and **per-street-mile** rank-correlate at
**Spearman ρ = +0.713** — the dense-core (Manhattan) toll survives an
exposure denominator; it is a real signal, not a resident-count artifact
(Midtown is #1 on both). The chapter reports the per-resident rate as its
spine metric for cross-axis comparability and flags the daytime-population
caveat for the commercial-core anchors.

## Perceived safety (overlay, not a headline axis)

The chapter's framing is *the danger you fear vs. the danger that harms you.*
A rigorous perceived-safety axis at CD scale is not available: source
verification (2026-07) found **no current, publicly-downloadable per-CD
perceived-safety survey**. NYCHVS 2023 asks a safety-rating item
(`SAFETY_RATE`) but only at borough geography; NYPD's Elucd "Sentiment Meter"
(2017–2020) measured neighborhood-level fear but was never released
publicly. The only genuine perception measure is DOHMH's Community Health Survey item
**`safeneighborhood16`** (2016: *"How safe from crime do you consider your
neighborhood to be?"*). But the CHS **public-use file omits the neighborhood
geography variable** (`uhf34` is Data-Use-Agreement-restricted, confirmed
absent from the 10,000-row public microdata) — so **no per-CD or per-UHF
perceived-safety rate is computable without a DUA request to DOHMH.** The
only figure available publicly is **citywide: 14.5% of adults rated their
neighborhood "not very" or "not at all" safe from crime (2016, weighted by
`wt17_dual`)** — a single number, not a map. The chapter therefore makes the
fear-vs-harm argument from *where the objective harm lands* (violent crime in
the feared districts, traffic in the calm ones) rather than from a measured
fear axis, and says so plainly. A UHF34→CD crosswalk is prepared
(`scratchpad/uhf_cd_crosswalk.csv`) should the DUA microdata be obtained
later; the overlay would then be a weighted mean of `safeneighborhood16 ∈
{3,4}` by UHF, joined to CDs.

## Caveats (full list; abridged in the chapter footer)

1. **Reported ≠ occurred.** NYPD complaint data is *reported* crime;
   under-reporting varies by crime type and by community trust in police, so
   a low violent-crime rate can partly reflect low reporting. Traffic KI is
   far less prone to this — a hospitalization or death is recorded regardless
   of trust.
2. **KI ≠ KSI.** No injury-severity grade in the public collision data, so
   the traffic axis is killed-or-injured, not the stricter killed-or-
   severely-injured Vision-Zero standard.
3. **Resident denominator ≠ exposed population.** Per-resident rates
   overstate risk in commercial cores (Midtown) and understate it where
   commuters/visitors are the victims. Cross-checked per street-mile.
4. **CD geography hides the intersection.** Traffic danger is a *corridor*
   phenomenon a CD average blurs.
5. **Perceived safety is proxied, coarse, and stale.** No current per-CD
   perceived-safety data exists; the CHS overlay is 2016, UHF-scale.
6. **A window, not a trend.** Three years stabilizes rates but says nothing
   about whether a place is getting safer.
7. **ZIP→CD approximation** in the street-mile denominator (ZIPs straddle
   CDs); adequate for a cross-check, not a headline number.
